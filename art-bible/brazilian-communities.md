# Shared direction — Brazilian communities

## Goal

Build specific, readable and inhabited places. Do not use “favela” as a random collection of brick, wires, water tanks and wear.

## Rules

1. **Place before props.** Define topography, climate, streets, construction history and land use.
2. **Human scale.** Doors, windows, stairs, sidewalks, furniture and vehicles share coherent measurements.
3. **Construction over time.** Variation comes from construction phases, available materials and use, not uniform noise.
4. **Resolved junctions.** Floor–wall, plaster–brick, window–wall, roof–parapet and street–sidewalk require intentional geometry and materials.
5. **Sidewalks and alleys are architecture.** They are not background textures or empty space between boxes.
6. **Localized wear.** Moisture, dust, impact and repairs respond to exposure and use.
7. **Connected infrastructure.** Wires, poles, pipes and boxes have origins, destinations and supports.
8. **A sky that belongs to the place.** Avoid zenith singularities, stretched clouds, generic haze and incompatible skylines.
9. **Purposeful props at coherent scale.** No miniatures, monoliths or objects unrelated to the scene.
10. **Mobile preserves identity.** Lower cost removes secondary detail, not the elements that define the place.

## Anti-references

- Vehicles with broken geometry.
- Miniature houses or shops placed on rooftops.
- Triangular or monolithic volumes without architectural purpose.
- Electrical networks drawn as unsupported straight webs.
- Generic red-dirt courtyards where constructed space is required.
- Identical blue glass repeated across every facade.
- Uniform peeling plaster covering entire walls.
- Legacy viewmodels without hands or outside the presentation standard.

## Model-independent process

GPT, Claude, GLM, Kimi, Mint and future tools follow the same references, contracts and gauntlets. Changing models never replaces a gate.
